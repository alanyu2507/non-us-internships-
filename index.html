Two words that decide everything

Read every Canadian posting for this

Canadian job ads carry two different requirements that look alike and aren't. "Authorized to work in Canada" is about your passport — you clear it, and it quietly removes every international student from the competition. "Enrolled in a Canadian university" is about your registrar, and it blocks you cold.

  • clearTesla, Synopsys, Qualcomm, Rockwell, Google, MicrosoftWork-authorization language only. Rockwell's co-op posting is explicit: legal authorization required, no sponsorship offered.
  • blocksAMD MarkhamPosting text reads "Enrolled in a Canadian University." Worth one recruiter email before you write it off — but don't build the plan around it.
  • blocksVector Institute, NSERC USRA, U of T ESROPAll reserved for students registered at Canadian institutions. Not a citizenship test — an enrollment one.
  • blocksMitacs Globalink, Fulbright–MitacsBoth exist to bring foreign students into Canada. Fulbright's version requires US citizenship outright.
  • watchTerm lengthCanadian co-op runs 4, 8, 12 or 16 months. Michigan gives you 12–14 weeks. Filter for the 4-month variant and say your window up front.
  • watchWaterlooWorks is shut to youIt's a closed portal for UW students, and a large share of Canadian co-op supply never leaves it. Everything you apply to has to come off a public careers board.

The China problem, stated plainly

Legal, but narrow

There is exactly one lawful route for you: an S2 visa carrying an 实习 ("internship") annotation, capped at 180 days, filed by the host company. Beijing's Zhongguancun program and Shanghai's S2 guidance both cover students enrolled at overseas universities listed on China's MOE registry — Michigan qualifies, and so do the China entities of Fortune Global 500 firms like Microsoft, Apple and Intel.

  • closedZ visa / work permitTier B needs a bachelor's plus two years' experience. A sophomore has neither.
  • closedCategory C youth internship permitThe one permit built for foreign interns runs on bilateral agreements — France, Germany, Singapore. Canada isn't on the list.
  • 2029K visaGenuinely liberal, no employer needed — but requires a completed STEM bachelor's. File this away for after you graduate.
  • illegalInterning on a tourist or business visa¥5,000–20,000 fines, 5–15 days detention, deportation with a ten-year re-entry ban. Someone will suggest it. Don't.

The visa isn't even the hardest part. Duration minimums and graduation-year windows kill more applications than immigration does — Qualcomm China wants six months, AMD China runs July–December at 3–5 days a week, and most 暑期实习 programs target the cohort graduating next year, not 2029. Apply where the channel is English and global (Apple, NVIDIA, MSRA), and raise the visa in the first recruiter call, not after an offer.

Two adjacent things worth naming. Nokia Ottawa and Ericsson Ottawa are among the strongest hardware co-op employers in Canada and are excluded here only because they're Finnish and Swedish — if the real constraint is "must be in Canada" rather than "must be American," they outrank half the Tier-2 list. And Huawei Canada's Markham lab is posting a "Co-op Researcher, Physical AI" role that is a near-perfect topical match for you — but Huawei sits under US sanctions, and that line on a résumé can complicate future clearance or export-controlled work at exactly the American robotics companies you're aiming at. Make that a decision, not an accident.

The gap in your stack

One thing to fix before December

Verilog, FreeRTOS, PCB and controls make you strong at Tenstorrent, AMD, Altera, Ciena, Kepler, MDA, Xanadu and Sanctuary. But nearly every Canadian robotics co-op posting — Rockwell/Clearpath, OTTO, Waabi, Avidbots — asks for ROS 2, C++, Python, Linux and Docker. Putting ROS 2 on the hexapod between now and December widens the funnel by roughly a third of this list, and it's the cheapest thing on your schedule.

Second move, higher leverage than any single application: Canadian professors can hire you onto grant money with zero immigration paperwork, and you cost them nothing an international student would. Cold-email robotics faculty at U of T, Waterloo, UBC, McGill and Sherbrooke in October–December with the hexapod, the FPGA work and the PCBs attached. Hardware depth is rarer than ML coursework in that inbox.
